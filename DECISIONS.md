## Insights and search

| Area | Decision | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insights, one page | This week (how much you wrote; a chart counting each day's activities that gave energy, up in blue, and used it up, down in red, never averaged, with a tapped day's entries and a table view; what stood out; the feelings that came up most), then Life areas (below), then the last 30 days in words (gives and uses up energy, when time flew, places, people and times, small joys), then Next steps and What you're learning (newest three), then Earlier weeks and Review a period. The energy parts come from activities, so since v0.15 they speak of earlier ones only (owner's choice: left as they are until step 2 gives Insights something to say about writing). Nothing to choose or fill in; every line opens to the entries behind it | TEST | Owner, 2026-10-23: "two graphs showing the same thing", "suboptions which I don't know is a better option" |
| What counts as a pattern | Ranked only with 3+ entries (`MIN_COUNT`), with the count. Places, people and times: 3+ entries that went one way 60%+ of the time, up to four. A line whose entries all sit behind a line already shown is left out. Worded "In what you've written, not causes." | ADOPT | One or two entries are anecdotes |
| Chart colours | Blue gave energy, red used it up (the validated diverging pair); text never wears the data colour. Orange and violet failed the validator | ADOPT | Readable with red-green colour blindness |
| Logs search | Every word of an entry and its answers; 60 days with entries at a time, then "Show earlier" | TEST | Apple Journal's most-cited gap |
| Search by meaning, on the phone | Finds entries about a question even with none of its words, in Logs and Review a period; its numbers also sort life areas (below). BAAI's bge-small-en-v1.5 (MIT, 8-bit ONNX, English) runs in a worker with onnxruntime-web 1.30.0 (one thread) and Hugging Face's tokenizer. Its files come only from this site: `npm run model` fetches a pinned revision, checks each SHA-256 and stores them gzipped (about 28 MB, git-ignored; the build refuses without them); downloaded once on first use, never precached. Each finished entry's meaning is kept in `vectors` (never in a backup; worked out again after a restore) and brought up to date before each search. Logs offers it on the first search, and Insights' life areas while it's off; its finds sit under the word matches as "Also about this" (closest three). Review a period: with a focus, "Only entries about this" (1 standard deviation above the rest, at most 20; with under 10 entries or none standing out, the closest three). More → Search by meaning turns it off and removes the download (and the numbers of areas and loops). Closeness is never shown; no vector database (40,000 entries compare in 15 ms) | TEST | Owner, 2026-10-24: kept on the phone. bge-small put an expected entry first for 8 of 8 test questions (two other models: 4 and 5). Rules tuned on fictional entries only: to judge on the owner's |
| Life areas | What you write sorts itself by what it's about: nothing to pick. Seven starter areas (Money, Work, Tech & learning, Mindset, Health, People, Home & errands) and 16 possible ones (Travel, Cooking, Music, Crafts, Art, Sport, Reading, Faith, Pets, Nature, Games, Films & TV, Community, Writing, Clothes & style, Going out), each shown only once 3+ entries fit it. The search-by-meaning model reads each area's general examples once (`src/domain/areas.ts`; the centres kept in `areaCentres`, never in a backup). An entry goes to the area it's clearly closest to, after taking away what all writing shares, or to none: a wrong area is worse than none. Under 4 words, none. Long writing read in pieces can be in two. Worked out while the app is open, a moment after a finished entry changes (drafts aren't read), and for every earlier entry the first time. Needs search by meaning on: Insights offers it. Shown as writing's title, and in Insights for the last 30 days: areas with 3+ entries and their count, most first; the rest on one line by name; every line opens to its entries. Closeness is never shown | TEST | Owner, 2026-10-24: "If I have to think about where a thought belongs, I won't record it"; chose a starter list plus areas found in the writing, and the area as the title. On a fictional journal of 120 entries: 98 right, 15 in none, 7 elsewhere; 28 of 34 unseen ones right, none elsewhere; 15 of 16 on other topics found (`areasModel.test.ts`). Grouping leftover writing failed (1 clean group in 5; travel entries share no word), so new topics come from the hidden list (owner's choice). Rules tuned on fictional entries only: to judge on the owner's |

## Never

Check every new feature against these. Some were tried or weighed and turned down: the details are in git history.

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| Ratings, scores, averages or scales, even worded ones | Owner: "I don't want any rating to be given"; natural responses only |
| Paywalls or subscriptions | The one complaint found for every major journaling app |
| Streaks, guilt or "x of 30" | Streak anxiety makes people quit |
| Nagging or push notifications | Reminders are calendar alerts you set; Web Push would need a server |
| Emoji moods; game loops (pets, points, energy meters) | Too generic to mean anything; a game loop rather than self-care |
| Anything leaving the phone without a tap: an AI reading each entry as it's saved, a server, prompts by WhatsApp | The founding privacy rule. Owner, 2026-10-24: keep it on the phone |
| Cloud backup or sync | Owner chose phone-only |
| Data loss | The most serious complaint about journaling apps; every upgrade is tested on a real old database |
| Crowded screens, long setup or a tips page | Word budgets guard this; the welcome, openers and empty states do the job of tips |
| Repeating prompts | Ours don't repeat within 30 days |
| Dark mode or an Appearance choice | Owner, 2026-10-24 |
| Swapping the hand-written CSS or the tested `Dialog` for a UI kit, drawer library or app generator (Ionic, Konsta, v0, bolt) | Each means rewriting every screen, and none runs iPhone Safari, where the bugs were |
